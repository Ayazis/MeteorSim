How this is modelled
  • The rate is how many meteors a dark-adapted observer would see over the whole sky under a pristine sky (limiting magnitude 6.5). A screen shows only part of the sky, so the counter shows how many land in your view. Pick Just this view to put all of them in front of you.
  • Meteors burn up about 100 km high. Their angular speed and length come from the shower's speed, their distance from you and their distance from the radiant. Near the radiant they're short and slow, and about 90° away they're longest.
  • Near the horizon meteors are up to ~1,100 km away and seen through more air, so they're fainter and fewer.
  • Brightness follows a population index of about 2.5: each step of one magnitude brighter is about 2.5× rarer. Bright, fast meteors can leave glowing trains that drift and fade.
  • Light pollution hides faint meteors and stars, just as it would for your eyes.
  • The stars, Milky Way and treeline are generated, not a real star chart.
