How this is modelled
  • The rate is how many meteors a dark-adapted observer would see over the whole sky under a pristine sky (limiting magnitude 6.5). A screen shows only part of the sky, so the counter shows how many land in your view. Pick Just this view to put all of them in front of you.
  • Meteors burn up about 100 km high. Their angular speed and length come from the shower's speed, their distance from you and their distance from the radiant. Near the radiant they're short and slow, and about 90° away they're longest.
  • Near the horizon meteors are up to ~1,100 km away and seen through more air, so they're fainter and fewer.
  • Brightness follows a population index of about 2.5: each step of one magnitude brighter is about 2.5× rarer. Bright, fast meteors can leave glowing trains that drift and fade.
  • Light pollution is set on the Bortle scale. Each class maps to a typical sky brightness (SQM, in mag/arcsec²), and that to the faintest star you can see. It hides faint meteors and stars, just as it would for your eyes.
  • The sky is computed for the chosen place and moment: sidereal time from the UTC date and longitude, and the observer's latitude. Each shower's radiant sits at its real position among the stars, so it rises and sets. A shower's rate scales with the sine of the radiant's altitude, as the ZHR does, so a radiant below the horizon gives almost no meteors. Scenarios jump to the shower's peak night, at the darkest hour with the radiant highest for your location.
  • The Sun and Moon come from low-precision ephemerides. Twilight and moonlight brighten the sky (and the brightness sets the limiting magnitude), so stars and meteors fade through astronomical, nautical and civil twilight until only fireballs show in daylight. The Moon is drawn with its phase, its lit side toward the Sun. The clock uses local standard time, without daylight saving.
  • The comet sits still among the stars, as a real one does over a night's watch. It isn't tied to the shower.
  • The stars and Milky Way are a real all-sky map (NASA's Deep Star Maps 2020, from Gaia, Hipparcos and Tycho data), turned to the horizon of the chosen site at the chosen time. Toward the horizon they dim and redden through more air, faint light loses its colour as it does for night vision, and stars twinkle more low down.
  • The comet stays fixed above the landscape rather than among the stars. The comet and the landscape are invented. Without WebGL2, or when the page is opened straight from disk, the stars and Milky Way are generated too.
